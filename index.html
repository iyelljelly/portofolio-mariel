Beyond the Gear

Group project PROGRESSIO · Semifinalist First-ever competition
The gist

We pitched a growth strategy for Torch, an urban gear brand, built around one idea: Gen Z doesn't just buy backpacks, they buy identity. So we proposed turning functional bags and accessories into identity-driven pieces through high-affinity IP collaborations.

My role

This was my very first competition ever, entered with team FIRSTIMERZ at the campus level. I dove into market and consumer research — digging into how Gen Z purchasing behavior actually responds to collab products — and helped shape the business logic and storyline behind the case.

The challenge

Torch needed to stand out in a crowded urban-gear market and win over a generation that buys identity as much as utility. Our brief: design a product and marketing strategy that could make that shift feel believable, not gimmicky.

What we proposed
  • ●An "Active, Integrate, Reliable" (A.I.R.) brand strategy — repositioning Torch from utility gear to an identity platform
  • ●Three IP-collaboration product lines, each paired with its own user archetype and story hook
  • ●A go-to-market and feasibility case backed by measurable lifetime-value and demand targets
Impact

We made it to the semifinalist round — a solid result for a first-ever competition, and proof that the research-driven approach we took actually held up under judging.

What I learned

This was my first real taste of business case competitions — and my first time dropping into a brand-new team on the fly. It taught me how to turn messy, open-ended consumer research into a tight, defensible business argument, a skill I've leaned on in every case study since.

Good Governance & Civic Tech

TITU — Play your city, own your track.

Group project I/O Festival 2026 (BEM FTI UNTAR) · Participant IS Case Study Club
The gist

TITU turns everyday Jakarta commuters on TransJakarta into city-builders — every ride feeds real CO₂ savings into a game where they build and grow their own miniature city. Yes, your commute can finally feel like it's doing something.

My role

Built with two classmates for I/O Festival 2026, guided by the Information System Case Study Club. I owned the concept and UI direction — shaping the Apple-inspired glassmorphism interface, designing the isometric pixel-art city assets, and helping turn "commuting = city-building" into an actual working prototype instead of just a nice slide.

The problem

Millions of people ride TransJakarta daily, but almost none of them can see how much carbon they're saving by choosing public transit over a private vehicle. We wanted to make that invisible impact visible — and genuinely rewarding, not just a stat buried in an app.

What we built
  • ●A CO₂-tracking engine that converts real commuting data into in-game currency
  • ●A city-building layer where that currency unlocks buildings, missions, and levels
  • ●An Apple-inspired glassmorphism visual language across onboarding, check-in, and tracking screens
  • ●Custom isometric pixel-art buildings and a full interactive prototype, packaged into our competition proposal
Impact

TITU went in as our full I/O Festival 2026 submission — a complete, interactive concept for making sustainable commuting fun, built end-to-end within the competition timeline.

What I learned

My second competition, and the one that taught me how much a clear timeline actually matters. We hit a genuinely chaotic time crunch mid-project — pushing through it taught me to lock down the idea and visual direction early, instead of letting both drift toward the deadline.

Caption: Wise — proposed for ISFEST, theme "The Psychology of Money"
ISFEST · Theme: The Psychology of Money

Wise

Group project ISFEST Competition UI/UX & Product Strategy
The gist

Wise is a behavioral-finance app concept built for ISFEST's "Psychology of Money" theme. Instead of just another expense tracker, it's designed around the actual reason most people struggle with money: not bad math, but bad habits, emotions, and mindset around spending.

The problem

Most people don't fail at managing money because they can't count — they fail because of emotional triggers, like stress-driven impulse buys, and a mindset about wealth that was never really examined. That gap between knowing and doing is exactly what standard budgeting apps tend to miss.

What we proposed
  • ●Emotion-based tracking — logging the mood behind a transaction to surface patterns of emotional spending
  • ●Mindset-building — bite-sized, gamified insights that teach the psychology of money without feeling like a lecture
  • ●Smart budgeting — reminders and goal visualizations that nudge users toward delayed gratification instead of impulse spending
My role

I led the UI/UX side — building wireframes and a high-fidelity Figma prototype with a clean, minimal, Apple-leaning glassmorphism look so a topic as heavy as "financial psychology" would feel approachable instead of intimidating. I also shaped the user journey around the Psychology of Money brief and put together the pitch deck for the judges, plus mapped the underlying system logic so every feature made sense end-to-end.

Impact

Wise went in as our full ISFEST submission — a complete concept and prototype that reframed "budgeting app" as something closer to a quiet, judgment-free coach for your money habits.

What I learned

Designing around a psychological theme instead of a technical one pushed me to think past screens and flows — every interaction had to actually feel non-judgmental, which changed how I wrote copy and chose colors, not just how I arranged buttons.
